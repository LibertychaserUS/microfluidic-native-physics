# Frozen expanded-analysis protocol

Status: locked before implementation of the expanded experiment suite  
Lock date: 2026-07-16  
Study type: retrospective, exploratory analysis of public historical data

## 1. Claim boundary

This study evaluates prediction reliability and selective decision-making on
public flow-focusing droplet data. It does not provide prospective or wet-lab
validation, and it does not establish manufacturing feasibility, stable flow,
or guaranteed performance for a new device.

The 64-row stress set has already been inspected during pilot analysis.
Consequently, the expanded analysis is not preregistered or confirmatory. The
purpose of this file is to prevent further outcome-driven changes: every method,
threshold, endpoint, and negative control listed below will be reported whether
or not it favors the proposed framing.

## 2. Research questions

1. How much does reported performance change when row-random evaluation is
   replaced by grouped evaluation and a mixed-source external stress set?
2. Do prediction intervals calibrated on the comprehensive data retain their
   empirical coverage across the four external source groups?
3. Can scores computed without external labels rank candidates by relative or
   normalized prediction risk, and what risk--coverage trade-off results?
4. In a retrospective target-search replay, do screening rules improve the
   precision of answered design queries without concealing their effect on
   query coverage, successful-target recall, and oracle regret?

## 3. Data partitions and statistical units

- Comprehensive data: 868 rows.
- External stress set: 64 rows from four source groups. It must not be described
  as 64 independent studies. The Lashkaripour subset contains the authors'
  blind-validation experiments and the other rows are literature-derived; the
  final manuscript will use the exact provenance reported by the source paper.
- Row-random 80/20 splitting is retained only to reproduce the published DAFD
  baseline.
- Primary internal generalization audit: grouped splitting by device geometry
  (orifice width plus four normalized geometry descriptors).
- Secondary grouping: device geometry plus viscosity ratio.
- Exact eight-feature duplicates are kept in the data audit and prevented from
  crossing the exact-input grouped split.
- The 197 double-emulsion experiments contribute paired inner- and outer-
  diameter rows (rows 0--196 and 671--867 in the downloaded comprehensive
  spreadsheet). Each pair is one experimental cluster. Group identifiers are
  built as connected components of the requested grouping relation and these
  DE-pair links, so an inner/outer pair can never cross a fit/test boundary.
- The 20 blind-validation rows generated by the source authors correspond to 10
  new DE flow conditions, each with paired inner and outer diameters. Those
  pairs are also treated as experimental clusters in uncertainty summaries.
- Repeated seeds measure algorithm/split sensitivity; they are not treated as
  independent experimental replicates.

## 4. Locked endpoints

### Primary reliability endpoints

1. Absolute normalized-target error.
2. Absolute relative diameter error.
3. Selective risk as a function of retained coverage and its numerical area
   (AURC), with the full curve released.
4. Source-macro results (equal weight per source) alongside row-micro results.

### Secondary reliability endpoints

- MAE and RMSE in micrometers.
- MAPE and R-squared, with R-squared supplementary for small/narrow sources.
- Reliable-prediction precision and recall at 10% relative error.
- Sensitivity at 5%, 15%, and 20% relative error and at 5, 10, 15, and 20 um
  absolute error. Absolute thresholds are explicitly secondary because they
  favor smaller droplets.

### Interval endpoints

- Empirical coverage at nominal 90% and 95%.
- Coverage gap from nominal.
- Mean and median absolute width in micrometers.
- Relative width (width divided by observed diameter) for retrospective
  evaluation.
- Overall and source-level values. Seed standard deviations are labeled as
  algorithmic sensitivity, not sampling confidence intervals.

### Inverse-design replay endpoints

- success@1, success@3, and success@5, where success means at least one selected
  candidate's observed diameter is within the target tolerance.
- Primary target tolerance: 10% of the requested target diameter.
- Secondary target tolerance: 10 um.
- Query coverage/abstention, successful-target recall among oracle-achievable
  targets, candidate count/reduction, achieved target error, and oracle regret.
- Precision among answered queries is never reported without query coverage and
  successful-target recall.

## 5. Model and split matrix

The published fixed-parameter XGBoost model is the focal reproduction model.
Random forest and histogram gradient boosting are model-robustness baselines;
external labels are not used to tune or select any model.

The following evaluation protocols are all reported:

1. 15 published-style row-random 80/20 splits.
2. 30 grouped splits by device geometry.
3. 30 grouped splits by device geometry plus viscosity ratio.
4. 15 exact-input grouped splits.
5. Full comprehensive-data training followed by the fixed external stress set.

For every split, split manifests and per-row predictions are retained.

## 6. Prediction intervals

The expanded analysis compares:

1. split conformal regression using absolute residual scores;
2. conformalized quantile regression (CQR);
3. normalized split conformal regression using a scale model trained from
   out-of-fold residuals before calibration.

Intervals are calibrated without external labels. Both row-random and geometry-
grouped calibration/test protocols are evaluated internally, followed by the
fixed external stress set. Empirical undercoverage under shift is described as
loss of transferability under non-exchangeable conditions, not as a failure of
conformal theory.

## 7. Applicability-domain and risk scores

All external scores are fitted without external labels. The complete comparison
contains:

- per-feature training min--max boundary;
- standardized k-nearest-neighbor distance with k in {3, 5, 10, 20};
- shrinkage Mahalanobis distance using OAS covariance;
- Isolation Forest anomaly score;
- Local Outlier Factor novelty score;
- One-Class SVM score;
- bootstrap-ensemble predictive disagreement;
- an out-of-fold residual-risk model fitted only on comprehensive-data folds.

Binary thresholds are learned from training-score quantiles
{0.90, 0.95, 0.975, 0.99}. No single threshold is called optimal on the external
set. Continuous risk--coverage curves are the primary analysis.

## 8. Negative controls and confounding checks

The following controls are mandatory:

1. random scores and random retention at matched candidate budgets;
2. within-source score permutation, preserving source composition;
3. hydraulic-diameter-only and predicted-size-only risk scores;
4. shuffled-label predictor sanity check;
5. comparison of absolute, relative, and normalized error outcomes;
6. overall and within-source rank associations;
7. explicit source retention tables for every binary rule.

If an applicability method does not outperform the size-only or matched-random
controls on relative/normalized risk, the manuscript will not claim candidate-
level applicability-domain benefit for that method.

## 9. Offline target-search replay

- The fixed engineering target grid is 30, 40, ..., 230 um.
- The primary replay uses a separate candidate pool for each external source so
  candidates from different fluid/device contexts are not mixed.
- A pooled candidate replay is supplementary and labeled as a sensitivity
  analysis only.
- Candidate outputs are hidden during ranking. Observed outputs are used only
  after selection to compute success and regret.
- Strategies: point prediction without screening, min--max boundary, each
  applicability score at all locked thresholds, interval-based target
  inclusion, and applicability-plus-interval screening.
- A strategy answers only when at least one candidate is predicted to fall
  within the requested tolerance; otherwise it abstains.
- Oracle-achievable targets are determined only for retrospective scoring and
  never used in candidate selection.

## 10. Inference and multiplicity

- Four source groups are too few for strong population-level inference.
  Source-cluster and hierarchical bootstrap intervals are descriptive and this
  limitation is stated explicitly.
- Row-level Wilson intervals may be shown only as conditional summaries, not as
  evidence of 64 independent experiments.
- Permutation tests are performed at the relevant source/query/group level.
- When families of method comparisons are tested, Benjamini--Hochberg adjusted
  values are reported together with unadjusted effect sizes.
- Effect sizes and full distributions take precedence over thresholded p-values.

## 11. Reproducibility gate

Results may be marked VERIFIED only after:

1. all data hashes and schema tests pass;
2. the experiment registry, split manifests, per-sample outputs, and figure
   source data are saved;
3. the complete suite finishes twice from separate clean artifact directories;
4. key scalar results agree within 5% relative difference (deterministic results
   should match exactly);
5. artifact hashes and software versions are recorded;
6. no external label is read by a fitting, calibration, threshold-selection, or
   candidate-ranking routine.

## 12. Prospective sealed-label blind case

The inspected DAFD external set cannot be relabeled as blind for this study.
One additional public experimental dataset that has not yet been used for model
or threshold development will therefore be handled with a sealed-label flow:

1. write and hash a dataset-specific protocol before target values are exposed;
2. acquire the raw artifact and record its URL, license, and SHA-256;
3. use a custodian step to expose schema and permitted predictor columns while
   keeping the target column out of the modeling workspace;
4. choose the held-out chemistry/device group by a deterministic hash rule,
   independent of target values;
5. fit the locked model and risk-screening workflow, save predictions, and
   record a prediction-file SHA-256;
6. reveal the target exactly once with a separate scoring command;
7. retain the pre-reveal protocol, feature manifest, prediction hash, reveal
   log, and all results, including a negative result.

The preferred case is the public surfactant-laden flow-focusing dataset linked
to DOI 10.1039/D2LC00416J, using an entire unseen surfactant/physicochemical
condition group as the blind holdout. If the artifact lacks the fields needed
for leakage-free group construction, the reason is documented and a compatible
public non-Newtonian or cross-device dataset is selected before any target is
opened.
